Savings Strategy Planner

A step-by-step helper for running the family gilt strategy. Nothing here places trades. It works out the numbers so you can check them before acting on your platform.

How to use this

  1. Load the platform downloads (button below). You can pick several files at once: each raw HL account download, the Barclays download, or the combined HL data workbook. Each file updates only the accounts it contains, so you can also refresh one account on its own. Funds, shares and cash are included in the totals.
  2. Holdings & prices: check the holdings. Prices update automatically from the gilt yield explorer whenever you open the app online, or press "Update prices" on tab 2. Otherwise, tell the app which account each gilt is in, then enter today's price (from your platform or the gilt yield explorer). If you're not sure, the value shown on the platform statement can be typed in instead.
  3. Overview: check the totals, how much is tax-wrapped and how much is taxable, and whether planned spending looks sustainable.
  4. Income for the year: set how much you want to spend. The app shows how much comes from gilt interest and how much to raise by selling small amounts of low-coupon gilts.
  5. April wrapper move: once a year, just after 6 April, the app works out what to sell in the taxable accounts and buy inside the ISAs/SIPPs.
  6. Save your work with the button below. Your entries are also remembered in this browser, but a saved file is safer.

Before acting on anything

The app gives estimates. Before any large trade, check the platform's own quote and settlement cash. If something doesn't make sense, stop and ask an adviser. With this strategy there is almost never a reason to rush.

Latest prices

Prices come from the gilt yield explorer's daily data (Tradeweb and HL), which updates automatically every afternoon. Index-linked gilts use the explorer's latest DMO index ratios.

Accounts

Each account is ISA (tax-free), SIPP (pension: tax-free growth but withdrawals are taxed), or Taxable (HL Fund & Share, Barclays Smart Investor). On first load, high-coupon gilts are placed in "Wrapped – to assign" and low-coupon gilts in "Taxable – to assign", following the strategy. Reassign each holding to its real account.

Holdings

Price is per £100 nominal, as quoted. For IL index-linked gilts, also enter the index ratio (the inflation uplift, e.g. 1.45; shown on the platform or DMO website). If you'd rather, type the platform's Value £, which overrides the price calculation.

Funds, shares & cash

Taken from the HL workbook and included in the totals. The strategy itself only trades gilts, so these are shown for completeness.

Is the spending sustainable?

Where this year's spending comes from

A · Move interest out of the wrappers (the "swap")

Interest paid inside ISAs and SIPPs can't be spent directly without losing the tax benefit (ISA) or paying tax (SIPP). Instead, use that cash to buy gilts inside the wrapper and sell the same amount of similar gilts in a taxable account. The taxable sale proceeds are what you spend. It's best done every few months, not every time interest arrives.

B · Top-up sales of low-coupon gilts

Low-coupon gilts pay little interest, but most of their return comes from the price rising towards £100 at maturity, and that gain is tax-free. The rule: for each taxable holding, you may sell about (spending rule % − its running yield) × value each year.

The annual April move (taxable → ISA/SIPP)

Each new tax year (from 6 April), move as much as the allowances permit from the taxable accounts into the ISAs/SIPPs, without ever being out of the market. Gilts maturing near April inside the wrappers provide the cash to buy straight away.

Allowances this tax year

ISA: up to £20,000 per adult per tax year (check for changes). SIPP: generally limited to that person's UK earnings for the year (£3,600 gross if no earnings), lower still once a pension has been flexibly drawn, and no tax relief from age 75. If Richard has died, Yvonne can also use an "additional permitted subscription" equal to the value of his ISA. Ask the ISA provider about this.

The strategy in plain English

  1. Mostly UK government bonds (gilts). Gilts pay a fixed interest ("coupon") twice a year and repay £100 per £100 nominal on a known date. Index-linked gilts rise with inflation.
  2. High-coupon gilts go inside ISAs/SIPPs, because their interest would otherwise be taxed.
  3. Low-coupon gilts go in the taxable accounts. Their return comes mostly from the price rising to £100, and gains on gilts are free of Capital Gains Tax.
  4. Each April, move as much as allowances permit from taxable to wrapped (tab 5).
  5. Through the year, spend taxable interest, sell small amounts of low-coupon gilts (tab 4B), and "swap" interest out of the wrappers (tab 4A).
  6. Keep it simple. Holding to maturity is always a reasonable default. Switching between short and long gilts to profit from interest-rate moves is optional, and only worth doing with professional advice.

Watch-outs

  • SIPPs are not tax-free income. Withdrawals above the 25% tax-free part are taxed as income. From 6 April 2027 unused pension funds are also due to count towards Inheritance Tax (anything left to a spouse is still exempt).
  • Cash inside a Stocks & Shares ISA: from 6 April 2027, interest on uninvested cash in a S&S ISA is due to carry a 22% charge, and an ISA can't hold only money-market funds. Park ISA cash in short gilts or a money-market fund alongside gilts.
  • Accrued Income Scheme: buying or selling gilts in a taxable account between coupon dates moves some interest between buyer and seller for tax. With holdings over £5,000 nominal this has to go on the tax return. Keep the contract notes.
  • Inflation: conventional gilts pay fixed amounts. Spending a fixed % each year from a mostly conventional portfolio steadily reduces what it can buy. See tab 3.
  • Gifts to Jamie: the money paid into Jamie's ISA and SIPP is a gift and legally becomes his. Gifts paid by selling investments (capital) usually count as "potentially exempt transfers". They fall out of the estate after 7 years. Each parent's £3,000 annual exemption also applies. The separate exemption for regular gifts out of surplus income needs evidence that the gifts come from income, not capital. Keep a record of each gift.
  • Probate: accounts in one person's sole name are frozen on death until probate. Keep enough cash in each person's own or joint name for several months of spending.
  • Check reference data: the list of gilts inside this app comes from the DMO list of gilts in issue. Before buying a suggested gilt, check that it still exists and is available on your platform.
