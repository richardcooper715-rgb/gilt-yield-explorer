Where these numbers come from
  • CNBC quotes six benchmark gilts (2, 5, 10, 30, 40 and 50 years) and says which gilt each one is. This page asks CNBC directly each time you refresh, so these points are as live as CNBC's own page. Each is plotted at its actual time to maturity.
  • MarketWatch (Tullett Prebon prices) covers 17 maturities from 1 to 50 years. Its quotes can't be read straight from a web page, so a GitHub job (live-quotes.yml) collects them every 15 minutes on weekdays during UK market hours. MarketWatch's own labels for the underlying gilts are out of date. So the job identifies each one as the gilt whose price/yield maths reproduces MarketWatch's price and yield together, and plots it at that gilt's real maturity. If one can't be identified, it's plotted at its nominal maturity (e.g. 10 years). GitHub sometimes runs these jobs late, and the status line shows when they were last collected.
  • The curve runs smoothly through the MarketWatch points (monotone cubic interpolation, so it never overshoots between them). If MarketWatch isn't available it runs through CNBC's points. These are gross redemption yields on coupon-paying gilts, not the Bank of England's zero-coupon curve shown in the Gilt Yield Explorer.
  • All gilts at last daily close are the yields in your daily gilt_latest.json update for every conventional gilt.
  • Both quote feeds are unofficial and free, so either could change or stop without notice. If one fails, the page shows the other.
